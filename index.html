The lifetime ledger

Stripped of prices, a life is a flow: things drawn from the planet and other people, and things returned. These are rough world-average totals over 73 years.

The lenses

Each lens answers a different question, which is why their numbers don't agree. None of them is wrong; they're measuring different things.

Planetary returns across 73 years

The orbital periods here are real astronomy. What they mean is astrology — a symbolic language, not a predictive science — but the timing lines up with life transitions often enough that people have used it as a calendar of meaning for millennia.

Sources and assumptions: global life expectancy 73.3 years (UN World Population Prospects 2024); US DOT value of a statistical life $13.2M in 2023 dollars; Australian Office of Impact Analysis VSL A$5.87M and A$253k per life-year in 2025 dollars; carbon at roughly 4.7 t CO₂ per person per year and the EPA's 2023 social cost of carbon estimate (~$190/t); other figures are rounded world averages meant for scale, not precision. Planetary periods: Jupiter 11.86 yr, Saturn 29.46 yr, lunar nodes 18.6 yr, Uranus 84 yr, Chiron ~50.7 yr.
